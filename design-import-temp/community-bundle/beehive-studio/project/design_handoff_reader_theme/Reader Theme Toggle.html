The stair had no bottom that Aldric could see, only a throat of black stone that swallowed the torchlight whole. Behind him the others waited at the threshold — Maren with her hand on the pommel she never drew, the boy Cael clutching the lantern as though it were the last warm thing in the world. None of them spoke. The catacombs of Varrock had a way of taking words and not giving them back.

He set his boot on the first step. Dust rose, fine as flour, and hung in the air long after it had any right to settle. The old maps named this place the Undercroft of the Sleeping Order, but the keepers above ground had a shorter name for it, one they only used after the third cup of ale: the Long Hush. Aldric understood now why they lowered their voices.

"The dead of Varrock do not rest," the abbess had told him, the morning he left. "They wait. There is a difference, and you will learn it before the week is out."

By the fortieth step the cold had become a living thing, pressing at the seams of his cloak, searching for a way in. Cael's lantern threw their shadows huge against the walls, and the shadows did not always move when they moved. Aldric chose not to mention this. Some observations, voiced, become true.

The Stairs Down

They found the first of the bone-doors at the landing, where the stair widened into a vaulted hall. It was exactly as the archivist had described: a slab of fitted femurs and fused skulls, mortared with something that gleamed faintly amber in the lantern-light, like honey gone to glass. Maren made a sound low in her throat that might have been a prayer or might have been a curse. In the catacombs the two were often indistinguishable.

"There's a seam," Cael whispered, and he was right — a hairline fracture ran the height of the door, and from it breathed a draft that smelled of old incense and older rot. Aldric pressed his palm flat against the bone. It was warm. Warm, forty fathoms beneath a city that had buried its kings in ice. Whatever lay beyond the door was not sleeping. It had simply been very, very patient.

· · ·

He drew his hand back and turned to the others, and for a moment the words would not come — the Long Hush had them, the way it had everything else down here. Then Maren drew her blade at last, and the ring of it was the loudest, most beautiful sound Aldric had heard in his life, and he found his voice in the wake of it.

"We open it together," he said. "Or not at all."
